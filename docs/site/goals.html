Goals and modes

What a hop is for, what each command's modes do on each side, and what the cutover adds when a hop turns it on.

Goals

A hop says what it is for in one word, its goal. What each does on the source and on the target, and what it guarantees, is what migkit init, migkit assess and every dry run say for the hop at hand.

Modes

CommandWhat it does
move --mode fullone copy of every table the hop carries: the source read, the target written - kept from the application's writes before the first row and again after the last where the hop asks (protect_target): its rights taken, its access policy changed on an answer of yes, or its writes named, the way the target allows
move --mode cdcthe stream of changes started, or resumed from where it stood: the source's log read, the target written change by change
move --mode full+cdcthe copy, then the stream from a position taken before the copy began, so nothing changed during it is lost
move --mode cdc --dropthe cutover: the stream torn down, the target given back to the application where protect_target kept it, and what else happens on each side is what cutover: says (below); run again once a cutover of the hop has flipped, its point of no return
sync --mode reconcilethe last check's differences shown; --apply writes the target to match the source
sync --mode verifyread only: every table compared as of one moment
sync --mode seedthe schema aligned, the copy, the differences repaired and a consistent check, one after another
sync --mode streamthe stream started, and each cycle's changes compared
sync --mode migrateseed, then stream

Without --go (or --apply) every one of them only shows its plan, starting with what each side will see.

The cutover, on or off

Off (no cutover: in the hop's options, whatever the goal): on the source, nothing written at cutover: the operator stops its writes and its jobs. In order:

  1. switch off on the source the jobs it runs (pg_cron jobs, MySQL events), so they write in one place
  2. stop the application's writes to the source: stop it, or revoke its write privileges and end its sessions - ending them is what rolls back what they had not committed
  3. migkit move HOP --mode cdc --drop --go - migkit proves nothing writes to the source and nothing is prepared there, waits for the stream to pass where the source stands, and only then tears it down (and starts the stream back, with reverse: at_cutover)
  4. migkit check HOP - the final comparison
  5. migkit sync HOP --go --kind sequences --tag pre-cutover - the target's counters past the source's
  6. switch the jobs on on the target
  7. point the application at the target
  8. once the application is on the target for good, the point of no return: drop the stream back, if one runs, and give the old source back its writes

On (cutover: true, or its settings): migkit does all of that itself, and adds:

  • a timed path: the write outage clocked from the pooler's pause or the freeze to the flip, against the hop's budget - past it, all of it is undone
  • the freeze: migkit stops the source's writes itself, the way chosen from what the source allows - the whole server made read-only where it may, or the application's accounts kept from writing and their sessions ended - and reads it back; PostgreSQL, MySQL, TiDB, CockroachDB, MongoDB, Redis, SQL Server, Oracle, Db2, SAP ASE, Teradata, ClickHouse, Cassandra, Couchbase, Kafka, OpenSearch, Redshift and Snowflake sources alike
  • the drain: the source's position taken after the freeze, and the hop's own stream waited for until it passes it - or, where the stream keeps no position migkit can read, a mark written on the source after the freeze and waited for on the target; a streaming pipeline and a leg run from here drained by their own offsets and positions
  • the fenced check: the rows changed since the last verified pass compared inside the outage, and the schema compared with the preflight's
  • the target's hold read back first (protect_target): the application's accounts write nothing there, or, where nothing holds a writer, the writes that are not migkit's named - one named is drift, and stops the cutover - and given back at the flip, put on again if the cutover is undone
  • the counters: the target's sequences and auto-increments raised past every key the source handed out
  • the stream back: from the new primary to the old source (reverse: at_cutover), made and proven before the freeze where its engine lets it wait idle, so the flip only starts it, and proven to reach the old source
  • the undo: every step recorded before it acts and put back, the last first, on any failure, on the budget passing, or on the next run after one killed half way
  • the outage measured: each run's outage and each step's time recorded, and the next cutover refused where the last one's was past its budget
  • the point of no return: a step of its own behind an approval of its own - the forward stream ended for good, the old source given back its writes, the stream back dropped, what the cutover left taken away - after which going back is refused

With it on, the source is written during the cutover only: its jobs switched off, its writes frozen, each step reversible and behind the hop's approvals; after the flip it stays frozen until migkit rollback HOP --db DB --apply, or until the point of no return gives it back.

Limits the move keeps to

Each is a hard limit: the plan keeps to it wherever some way can, and where none can it says what binds and what would help. A hop that sets none is planned for the soonest end.

  • deadline: the whole move - copy, index builds and check - planned to end within it; where no plan can, what binds is said and what would help
  • source_load: the share of the source's reads the move may take: the source is planned as if it read only that fast, the ways that read it less are preferred where that ends sooner, and while it runs the pace holds the move's reads at that share
  • replica_lag: the seconds the source's replica may fall behind while it is read: the pace holds the move to it
  • money: what the move may spend on what it carries (at the hop's transfer_price_per_gb) and on what a warehouse bills: the ways that bill less are taken first where the budget would be passed
  • bytes_billed: the bytes a warehouse may bill the move for: read as money at its price
  • outage: the cutover's write outage, seconds: the plan says whether its predicted outage keeps within it (with cutover: on, the cutover's own budget is this limit)
  • target_memory: the target's memory the move may take: as many writers at once as it holds
  • target_connections: the target's connections the move may take: as many writers at once as they carry
